L.02

What to send us

  1. The recording. The audio file as you received it, with the decision or from My Case: a link to your own storage, or our encrypted transfer.
  2. The passages. The start and end time of each one on the recording, or “the whole hearing”. A landmark helps: a question, a break, a change of speaker.
  3. The due date. The day your appellant’s record must be filed.
  4. The names. The spelling of the people and places that come up.
  5. Your file reference. It goes on the invoice.

We acknowledge the same business day, then confirm a fixed price and a delivery date in writing once we have heard the passages.

Request a quote or write to secure@lexoral.org.

L.03

Through an interpreter

Many claimants testify through an interpreter. We transcribe everything said in English or French: the member, counsel, and the interpreter’s rendering of each question and answer. What the claimant says in their own language is not transcribed; we mark where it occurs, so the passage can be found in the audio.

When a French-speaking claimant testifies through an interpreter at a hearing held in English, or the reverse, both languages are transcribed, the testimony and the interpretation each attributed to its speaker, so that one can be checked against the other.

L.04

The deadlines

At the Refugee Appeal Division, the appellant’s record is due 45 days after the written reasons are received (IRB practice notice). A transcript is not required; if the appeal relies on one, it goes in the record with the transcriber’s declaration.

SOR/2012-257

“… together with a declaration, signed by the transcriber, that includes the transcriber’s name and a statement that the transcript is accurate”

Refugee Appeal Division Rules, SOR/2012-257, para. 3(3)(b): the appellant’s record

At the Federal Court, an application for leave is perfected within 30 days, with the facts verified by affidavit (rule 10 of the Federal Courts Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection Rules). The tribunal’s record follows only once leave is granted (rule 17): if the hearing matters to the leave application, the transcript has to be ready within those 30 days.

L.05

Confidentiality

Proceedings before the Refugee Protection Division and the Refugee Appeal Division are held in the absence of the public (Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, s. 166(c)). We treat the recording accordingly: kept in restricted-access storage and deleted 30 days after delivery, under our retention policy.

L.06

Questions

Does your declaration meet the Refugee Appeal Division Rules?
It gives the transcriber’s name and states that the transcript is accurate, which is what paragraph 3(3)(b) of the Rules sets out. Whether your appeal needs more is for you to judge; we do not give legal advice.
Can you transcribe only part of the hearing?
Yes. Paragraph 3(3)(b) speaks of all or part of the transcript. Send the start and end time of each passage: the quote covers those passages only.
Can the transcriber swear an affidavit for the Federal Court?
Yes. On request, the transcriber swears or affirms an affidavit before a commissioner for oaths, with the transcript attached as an exhibit.
Do you transcribe the claimant’s own language?
Only French and English. When a claimant testifies in another language through an interpreter, we transcribe the interpretation and mark where the claimant speaks.
Do you work outside Quebec?
Yes. We work from the recording, whichever IRB office or virtual hearing room it came from, anywhere in Canada.
Do you work with legal aid offices?
Yes. Each file is invoiced separately, under your file reference, so the disbursement is simple to approve.
