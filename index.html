[Why Lexoral]

The record is the evidence.
Not a version of it.

Two languages, two different things in the file. One is testimony. The other is a rendering of it.

Under a section 530 order the law does not ask for a readable English document. It asks for the words as spoken, any interpretation as a separate transcript, and the exhibits in the language they were tendered. Most firms deliver the first thing.

What the witness said 
A.   Je n’ai jamais vu l’annexe C avant     le douze janvier.

The answer, as given, under oath. Subparagraph (i).

Interpretation only 
A.   I never saw Schedule C before     January twelfth.

Composed by the interpreter, and all a U.S. reporter is instructed to take down. Not the record subparagraph (i) describes.

Lexoral record 
A.   Je n’ai jamais vu l’annexe C avant     le douze janvier.     (Interpretation: I never saw     Schedule C before January 12.)

Both, each marked for what it is. Subparagraphs (i) and (ii) together — the record the statute describes.
