[The transcript]
II

v.

The record s. 530.1(g) requires.

For a trial held in the accused’s language, the Criminal Code sets out what the record must contain: everything said, in the official language in which it was said; the interpretation, transcribed separately; and the exhibits, in the language in which they were tendered. We apply that standard to every file, in every forum.

  1. iVerbatimEverything said, in order, including objections and crosstalk.
  2. iiOriginal languageFrench in French, English in English; interpretation transcribed separately and identified.
  3. iiiAttestedPaginated, line-numbered, signed by a named practitioner with a declaration of accuracy.

Filing in Quebec

Certified transcripts, by an official stenographer.

A transcript to be filed before a Quebec court is prepared and certified by an official stenographer accredited by the Barreau du Québec, who holds the mandate and bills you at the official tariff. Lexoral works in collaboration with an official stenographer accredited in French and English, and prepares the bilingual transcript under the stenographer’s mandate and responsibility.

  1. Request submitted to Lexoral.
  2. Mandate held by the official stenographer; bilingual transcript prepared by Lexoral.
  3. Certification by the official stenographer, against the recording.

Principal courts

III

Lex

Cited by page and line.

The transcript is what counsel cite, what the trial judge relies on and what the court of appeal reads. The rules it must meet depend on the forum: Quebec, the rest of Canada, the United States, or several at once.

Quebec

Civil law · both languages of record

The texts

  • Constitution Act, 1867, s. 133English or French before the courts of Quebec.
  • Code of Civil Procedure, art. 221Examination before trial, written or oral.
  • CQLR, c. B-1, r. 13Stenographers accredited by language.

Leading case

A.G. (Quebec) v. Blaikie, [1979] 2 S.C.R. 1016Section 133 binds the courts of Quebec.

Canada

Common law and federal · the accused’s language

The texts

  • Criminal Code, ss. 530 and 530.1(g)Trial in the accused’s language; a record in both.
  • Canadian Charter, s. 14The right to the assistance of an interpreter.
  • Official Languages Act, s. 14English or French before the federal courts.

Leading case

R. v. Beaulac, [1999] 1 S.C.R. 768The choice of language belongs to the accused, whatever their fluency in the other.
